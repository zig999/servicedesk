# The ask that closes this initiative

The human asked, in their own words:

> iniciativa pode ser fechada? sem sim, feche iniciativa

They were shown what disk held at the moment of asking — thirteen of thirteen tasks with both an
implementation record and a proof record over the `frontend` target, a plan that validates against
the specification at twenty nodes, a delivery that validates at twenty-nine nodes with three
reviews, a work root under git and clean, and no `closure.md`.

They were also shown two consequences of closing, and closed anyway.

The first is that a closed plan takes no new source: the thirty-one findings the review
`scope-review-corrections` recorded stand unacted, and acting on any of them now requires a new
initiative, where what this plan delivered returns as inventory through the survey of the code
itself.

The second is that `deliver.py --outstanding` refuses over the `backend` target, because every one
of the twenty-nine records spells the standard's path relative to the `frontend` target it was
delivered into. That refusal is not a defect of the work and bars no closure — this initiative
delivered into `frontend` alone, and the seal is `plan.py`, not `deliver.py` — and it stays in
history exactly as it stands.

They chose the why from options offered:

> A iniciativa está encerrada porque entregou tudo o que o escopo pediu: as treze tarefas estão
> implementadas e provadas, e as três revisões estão registradas. Não há mais tarefa a entregar
> contra este plano.
