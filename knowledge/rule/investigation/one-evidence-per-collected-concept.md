---
title: One evidence per collected concept
summary: The evidence of an investigation is total over what its case collects — exactly one per concept, whatever happened to the attempt.
ddd: invariant
statement: An investigation MUST record exactly one evidence for every concept its case's hypotheses collect.
sources:
  - intake/decisoes-cinco-perguntas-2026-08-06.md
constrains:
  - definition/investigation/investigation
  - definition/investigation/evidence
consistency: immediate
examples:
  - Given a case whose hypotheses collect three concepts, when the investigation is written, then it records exactly three evidences, one per concept.
  - Given a concept two hypotheses collect, when the investigation is written, then it records one evidence for that concept, not two.
---

## What it is

Totality is what makes the fallback selection readable from the evidences alone — every evidence carrying ok can only mean every fact arrived if every concept has an evidence.
An evidence missing would read exactly like a fact that arrived fine, which is the silence this rule refuses.

None.
